Services

Four piling methods. One goal — a foundation that holds.

We match the method to your soil report and structural load, not the other way round.

S 01 — CAST-IN-SITU ROTARY HYDRAULIC PILE

Cast-in-Situ Rotary Hydraulic Pile

A rotary hydraulic rig bores the pile shaft to the required depth, and the pile is reinforced and poured directly in place. It's a strong, durable foundation system suited to heavier structural loads and larger commercial or industrial builds.

  • High load-bearing capacity for multi-storey and industrial structures
  • Precise, vertical boring with rotary hydraulic equipment
  • Reinforcement and concrete pour completed on site
S 02 — BORED DIRECT MUD CIRCULATION PILE

Bored Direct Mud Circulation Pile

Drilling fluid is circulated through the bore to keep loose or water-bearing soil stable while drilling. This keeps the shaft from collapsing before reinforcement and concrete go in, making it a dependable choice on difficult ground.

  • Maintains bore stability in loose, sandy or waterlogged soil
  • Reduces the risk of shaft collapse during drilling
  • Suited to sites with a high water table
S 03 — UNDER-REAMED PILE

Under-Reamed Pile

The pile shaft is widened into a bulb at the base, spreading the structural load over a larger bearing area. This design performs well in expansive, black cotton soils that shrink and swell with moisture.

  • Wider base bulb increases load-bearing area
  • Well suited to expansive and shrink-swell soils
  • Reduces uplift and settlement risk over time
S 04 — LOAD TEST ON PILE

Load Test on Pile

Before the structure above is built, we test installed piles under controlled load to confirm they perform to the design specification. It's the final check that turns a poured pile into a verified foundation.

  • Confirms load-bearing performance against design values
  • Identifies any settlement or performance issue early
  • Documentation provided for your structural engineer
